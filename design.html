Game Design Document · v0.1

LAST MILE — a post-apocalyptic delivery roguelike

You are a courier at the end of the world. The outposts that still exist are far apart, the roads are gone, and the only thing anyone still trades is what someone can carry to them. Every run is one expedition: walk out, decide what's worth dying for, reach the beacon.

Genre traversal roguelike Platform browser (Three.js) Camera third-person orbit Run length 8–20 min Status design
01

Pitch

A walking game where the walk is the fight. Carry too little and you starve on payout; carry too much and your own legs become the enemy. The world doesn't chase you — it just keeps being bigger than you, and the storm keeps telling you how much time you have.

One sentence: Death Stranding's lonely weight-and-terrain walk, compressed into a twenty-minute roguelike run with a shrinking clock.

Rule: the world is desaturated — mud, moss, concrete, fog. Only two things are allowed saturated colour: amber (safety, the beacon, your visor) and teal (loot). Red is reserved for the hound.

Touchstones

◐

Feel — the weight of a body

Death Stranding's cargo-and-balance anxiety; A Short Hike's small-scale walkability; the Long Dark's sense that weather is an opponent, not a backdrop.

▤

Look — quiet apocalypse

Not rubble-porn. Hills that look like they've healed over the ruins. Empty roads. Tall grass moving in wind. One brutalist outpost silo on the horizon.

≋

Sound — wind, boots, metal

Wind as the bed. Footsteps that change with terrain. A low sub-bass swell when the storm turns toward you. No music during the walk; a single rising drone at delivery.

⌁

Touch — honest friction

Turning is slow. Sprinting costs. Landing hurts. Every input is answered by a body that has mass, and every mistake is legible: you can see why you fell.

03

Design pillars

01

The body is the mechanic

Weight, balance, stamina and footing are the combat system. Nothing attacks you as hard as your own greed does.

02

Every meter is readable

If a system can kill you, you can see it: balance sways visibly, cargo sags, the wall's distance is always on screen. No hidden dice.

03

Distance is the enemy

Encounters exist to make distance cost something — never to be the point. You lose by running out of road, not by losing a fight.

04

Runs are decisions, not dice

Randomised terrain and loot, but the run's shape comes from what you chose to carry, where you chose to cut, and when you chose to rest.

04

Core loop

SURVEY — see the beacon, read the terrain → WALK — spend body, cross ground → LOOT? — the greed choice → SURVIVE — storm, hounds, terrain → DELIVER — payout + rating → UPGRADE — pick 1 of 3 → PUSH FURTHER

The loop is deliberately short and the beat between Loot and Survive is where the game lives: every crate is a small bet that the rest of the trip is worth less than the crate. Difficulty rises per delivery — bigger map, faster wall, more hounds — so the bet gets worse exactly as the payout gets better.

06

Gameplay elements

Cargo CORE

Up to N crates on your back. Each adds payout value, slows you, raises balance load and makes you louder. Visible stacked on the walker.

Balance & tripping CORE

A sway meter that falls under load, slope, sprint and water. At zero you trip — drop a crate, take the hit, lose condition. The signature failure.

Cargo condition CORE

Starts at 100%. Falls from falls, hits, storms. Multiplies payout — a perfect walk pays double a battered one.

Stamina CORE

The body's budget. Sprint and climbing spend it, resting returns it, exhaustion locks you out of anything faster than a walk.

The storm wall CORE

A visible, closing weather front. Outrun it or it costs you; it is the run's clock expressed as something you can see coming.

Hounds ENEMY

Hunt by noise, not sight. Carry more and they find you sooner. They don't kill you quickly — they cost you cargo and condition.

Salvage LOOT

Teal crates scattered in the field. The greed loop: each one you take is a decision to make the rest of the trip harder.

Contracts STRUCTURE

Each delivery has a type: Standard, Express (a clock), Fragile (damage halves pay), Heavy Haul (slower, richer).

Rating FEEDBACK

S / A / B / C from condition, time and damage taken. The score is the story of how you walked.

Upgrades META

Three random offers per delivery from a pool that patches weaknesses: grip, boots, casing, quiet tread, stamina, cargo slots.

Terrain WORLD

Endless procedural hills, rivers and ridges, streamed in chunks. Every slope you can see, you can climb — it just costs.

Gait telemetry EXPORT

Per-frame joint angles, speed and slope recorded on demand — the walker is modelled on humanoid hardware and its motion is capturable.

07

The walker

The player body is a jointed humanoid — hip, knee, ankle, foot per leg; shoulder and elbow per arm — posed by an analytic gait cycle rather than baked animation. It's parameterised in real units (stride length in metres, cadence in cycles per second, joint angles in radians) around an average adult humanoid platform: ~0.72 m stride, ~1.9 cycles/s at cruise, ~60° peak knee flexion in swing, counter-rotating arm swing.

Because the gait is a function of speed, slope and load, it naturally produces the variation a learning dataset wants: slower and deeper-kneed uphill, shorter stride under cargo, wider sway when unbalanced. Press T in-game to record frames to window.WALKER_TELEMETRY — timestamp, speed, slope and all twelve joint angles.

Side objective This motion data is intended as a source for humanoid robot policy training. The game itself stays honest: nothing is faked for the dataset — the gait is the same one the player feels, so recorded motion is motion a human actually drove.

Progression & difficulty

DeliveryWhat changesWhy
1–2Short distances, one hound, small stormTeach the body: weight, slope, stamina
3–5Longer legs, contract variety, second houndIntroduce the greed decision with real stakes
6–10Faster wall, more storms, deeper waterForce route choices — the straight line stops being viable
11+Hound packs, scarce beacons, brutal terrainEndgame is a war of attrition against your own greed

No permanent upgrades between runs in v1 — the skill you build is knowing when to stop carrying. Meta-progression, if added, should be cosmetic or contract unlocks, never power.

09

Interface principles

  • All UI is drawn in canvas — no HTML overlays. HUD, menus, upgrade picks and death screen share the render surface.
  • Left: the body. Hull, stamina, balance, boots, cargo count and condition — always in the same place.
  • Right: the run. Score, deliveries, combo streak.
  • Top centre: the world. Compass to the beacon, distance in metres, hound bearings on the same ring.
  • Nothing modal during play. Messages fade; the upgrade picker is the only pause.
10

Tech & v1 scope

Single-file Three.js game, static-hosted (GitHub → Cloudflare Pages). Chunked terrain generated from an analytic height field so the walker's feet and the ground can never disagree. Audio is synthesised at runtime — no assets. No build step.

In v1

  • Endless terrain, water, storms, hounds, salvage, four contract types
  • Balance/trip, cargo condition, boot wear, stamina, crouch
  • S/A/B/C rating, 12+ upgrades, permadeath with session best
  • Canvas HUD, synth audio, gait telemetry export

Deliberately out of v1

  • Online structures, likes or any social layer
  • Vehicles, NPCs, dialogue, story beats
  • Permanent meta-progression
11

Open questions

Is the storm wall better as a hard boundary (death) or a soft one (drain)? Soft feels fairer; hard creates real urgency.
Should dropping cargo be free, or cost a beat of vulnerability? Free makes the greed choice crisper; a beat makes it tense.
Does the walker need a visible "stagger" animation at low balance, or is the sway enough signal?
How much of the map should be visible from the beacon's height — is the beacon a landmark you can always see, or a compass only?
Do hounds ever become a fight you can win, or are they purely a tax? Purely a tax is more honest to the pillars.
